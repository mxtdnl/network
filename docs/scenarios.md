# Scenario projects

Seven fictional projects that model the conditions of real survey data, for testing Graticule and for trying it on something other than the demo. Each one is built to contain one condition. Like the demo, they are generated by a seeded script (`scripts/generate-scenarios.ts`, `npm run scenarios`), and a unit test checks that each committed file is byte-identical to the generator's output and still holds the condition it was built for (`tests/unit/scenarios.test.ts`). `tests/e2e/scenarios.spec.ts` opens each one in the built site.

**To load one:** download the file from `src/demo/` and choose Project → Open project… in Graticule. Every name pairs a first name with a term from a different field per scenario (nautical, typographic, agricultural, astronomical, geological, meteorological and river terms), so none can be taken for a real person. Every tie is marked as imported.

| File | Members | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| `scenario-partial-response.ona.json` | 18 in 3 teams | 15 responded; the three who did not include the unit's most connected member; one respondent stopped after 8 of 17 colleagues |
| `scenario-asymmetric-ratings.ona.json` | 20 in 4 teams | Ratings differ by direction: seniority, a generous rater, a harsh rater, warmth one way and coolness the other, reliance one way only |
| `scenario-compressed-ratings.ona.json` | 24 in 3 teams | 17 respondents avoid both ends of every scale (2–4 of 0–5, never negative valence); 3 give everyone the same answer; 4 use the whole scale |
| `scenario-matrix-organisation.ona.json` | 30 in 5 functions | Functions (the team attribute) and cross-functional programmes cut across each other; work follows the programme; two contractors have no function |
| `scenario-large-department.ona.json` | 64, one team value | No team below the department; ties follow seating, shifts and supervisors; strangers are rated 0, and valence for them is "does not apply" |
| `scenario-dense-team.ona.json` | 18 in 4 disciplines | Everyone works closely with everyone: connection strength 3–5 for every pair |
| `scenario-post-merger.ona.json` | 36 in 4 teams | Two legacy organisations with different rating habits; an integration lead sought for advice; a new joiner; energy and conflict layers; four partial responders |

The project notes in each file (`meta.notes`) list the members who carry each condition.

## What the tool shows with each

Measured on 2026-10-22 with the engine and the app's default settings (default weights, signed layers as multipliers, directed view, threshold 0.30), unless another view is named. Screenshots, with names hidden, are in `docs/screenshots/scenarios/` (`SCENARIO_DIR=docs/screenshots/scenarios npx playwright test tests/e2e/scenarios.spec.ts`).

### 1. Partial response (Harbour Survey Unit)

- Coverage is 80.1 % against the default threshold of 80 %, so no coverage warning is shown although 3 of 18 members gave no ratings. The Coverage tab lists CHA05, FIE01 and FIE04 at 0 % and HYD06 at 47 %.
- FIE01, the Field Operations lead, receives the most (in-strength 12.3, twice the next member's 6.1) but has out-strength 0 and betweenness 0 in the directed view, because a non-respondent has no outgoing ties. The member panel and metrics table show these zeros like any other value: nothing marks them as non-response.
- The directed view names HYD01 as the only potential broker. The symmetrised view fills each missing direction from the one given (plan Q3), under every rule including Weaker of the two, and FIE01 becomes the only potential broker (betweenness 0.65). Which view is chosen therefore decides who appears to bridge the teams.

### 2. Asymmetric ratings (Lantern Studio)

- Tie reciprocity is 0.82 and dyad reciprocity 0.70.
- DES04, who rates everyone generously, has out-strength 18.2 (next highest 10.6) and is named a potential broker in the directed view and when the two directions are averaged; with Weaker of the two they are not. ENG05, who rates everyone harshly, is listed as a peripheral member in both symmetrised views, from their own ratings.
- The director gives an average out-strength of 3.6 and receives 10.2.
- The three pairs who rate each other warmly one way and coolly the other are not a negative cluster (correct: the rule needs negative ratings in both directions). The cool direction's composite weight is 0.00–0.06, because the negative valence multiplies it down, so it falls below the 0.30 threshold and its negative rating is never drawn. No view or insight points to one-way negative ratings.

### 3. Compressed ratings (Granary Cooperative)

- The composite's density is 0.98 and 274 of the 276 pairs are drawn at the default threshold, because a rating of 2 out of 5 rescales to 0.40. The map is a uniform mesh.
- Louvain still recovers the three teams exactly, but with modularity 0.08.
- The three members who gave everyone the same answer have the three highest out-strengths (16.1 each), and two of them are named potential brokers with betweenness of at most 0.04.
- The strained relationships among four Bakery members do not appear as a negative cluster: only BAK03 uses negative ratings.
- Nothing in the tool shows a respondent's spread of answers, so straight-lining and scale compression can only be found by reading the matrix.

### 4. Matrix organisation (Compass Partners)

- Communities follow the programmes: 3 communities, 90 % of members in their community's majority programme, against 30 % for function.
- The silo rule finds nothing (correct: every function spans the programmes). The formal–informal rule finds formal-only ties within Design and Data (and Engineering when symmetrised by Weaker of the two): the chapter meetings and line management.
- The potential brokers are the members on two programmes (ENG05, DES03) and the coordinator (SHA02).
- The insight rules group members by the team attribute only (`INSIGHT_RULES.silo.attributes`), so silo and formal–informal questions are never asked of programmes. The map can still fill and group by programme.
- The two contractors with no function are shown as "Not recorded".

### 5. Large department (Ledger Operations Centre)

- The silo rule reports the whole department as a silo: "Operations", all 64 members, 822 ties inside and 0 outside (E-I −1.00), with the question "How does work reach the rest of the organisation?". With only one team value there is no outside, so this is an artefact of the rule (see the assessment below).
- Louvain divides a continuous seating gradient into 6 communities with modularity 0.34. They follow location (84 % purity) more than shift (67 %); they are cuts through a gradient, not teams.
- The peripheral rule lists 13 members, including all 8 remote workers (the list has no cap, unlike the brokers rule's 5).
- No member names are drawn on the map, because more than 60 members are shown (D53). 3,035 ratings are "does not apply"; coverage is 94.7 %.

### 6. Dense team (Tideway Product Team)

- Density 1.00; betweenness is 0 for everyone, so no potential brokers (correct); one community with modularity 0 (correct).
- The peripheral rule still lists three members (PRO02, DES04, RES01) whose in-strength (14.8–15.0) is within 10 % of the highest (16.4), because it takes the bottom 20 % of members whatever the spread.
- Constraint runs from 0.221 to 0.223, yet the table ranks every member on it. Rank ranges from the stability tool are the way to show that such ranks are not meaningful.

### 7. Post-merger integration (Confluence Group)

- The structures built in are found: INT01 under possible overload (workflow dependency in-degree 23 against a median of 6), and the four Technology members across the legacy line as a negative cluster on both valence and energy.
- The conflict layers are summed into the composite with weight 1 each, like any unsigned layer (D26), so with the default weights each makes up 0.14 of the composite: more friction makes a stronger composite tie.
- All four silo observations come from the conflict layers, e.g. "Most conflict: personal friction ties involving Technology stay within Technology: 7 inside and 0 outside". Conflict among people who work together stays inside teams by nature; it is not a silo.
- Northbank respondents give a mean connection strength of 2.05 and Southbank 0.88, while the two groups receive 1.49 and 1.40. Out-strength therefore measures the rater's habits as much as their connections. Communities follow team (100 %) rather than legacy organisation (56 %).

## Assessment

On 2026-10-22, before any of these files were added, the build was clean: typecheck, lint and Prettier passed, and all 1,191 unit tests in 28 files passed. Each scenario opens through Project → Open in the production build with no console or page error, and each analyses in both views without an engine warning.

What works well with this data:

- Every structure built into the scenarios that the rules are meant to find is found: the overloaded lead, the negative cluster, chapter-style formal-only ties, the members who bridge two programmes, and the remote workers on the edge of the large department.
- The rules stay quiet where they should: no brokers and one community in the dense team, no silo in the matrix organisation, and no negative cluster from one-way negativity.
- "Does not apply", declined ratings, missing attributes and partial responses are all carried through to coverage and the metrics without errors.

Issues found, most serious first. None has been changed: each touches a rule or default the owner approved, so each is a proposal.

1. **The silo rule fires on a group that holds every member** (scenario 5). With one team value, the E-I index is −1 by construction. Proposal: skip the rule when the attribute has fewer than two groups of at least `minGroupSize`, or when a group has no members outside it to tie to.
2. **The silo rule reads the conflict layers** (scenario 7). It runs on every unsigned layer (D72), and the conflict sub-layers are unsigned frequencies, so friction that stays within teams is reported as a silo, sometimes from as few as 7 ties. Proposal: leave layers in the `conflict` group out of the silo rule (as D105 already treats them with the signed layers for exports), and set a minimum number of ties per group and layer.
3. **Conflict raises the composite** (scenario 7). Enabling the conflict layers adds them to the composite with weight 1. Proposal: default weight 0 for the conflict group, or treat them like a signed layer's negative side.
4. **Non-response looks like no ties** (scenario 1). A member who gave no ratings has out-strength, out-degree and directed betweenness of 0, shown as real values, and the symmetrised view fills their row from others' ratings even under Weaker of the two. A coverage of 80 % can hide three complete non-respondents of 18. Proposals: a per-member flag ("gave no ratings" or "gave fewer than x % of ratings") on outgoing metrics in the member panel, table and exports; and a coverage note that counts complete non-respondents, not only the overall rate.
5. **Relative rules fire without an absolute floor** (scenarios 3 and 6). Peripheral members are the bottom 20 % even when everyone is within 10 % of each other; potential brokers are named at betweenness 0.04. Proposal: a minimum spread or minimum betweenness before an observation is made.
6. **Rater habits are not visible** (scenarios 2, 3 and 7). Generous, harsh, compressed and same-answer respondents shape out-strength, brokers and peripheral lists, and the tool has no view of each respondent's distribution of answers. Proposal: a per-rater summary in the Coverage tab (mean, spread, share of the scale used, and a note when every answer is the same). The analyst can already reduce the effect with Weaker of the two.
7. **Groupings beyond team are not used by the rules** (scenarios 4 and 7). Silo and formal–informal questions are asked only of the team attribute. Proposal: let the analyst choose the grouping attribute for the rules.
8. **Copy:** the silo sentence reads "Most composite of all layers ties involving Operations…" when the layer is the composite.

Smaller observations: at 1440 × 900 the compact layout's drawer covers the right edge of the large department's map until the view is fitted again (by design, D125); with more than 60 members no names are drawn until members are highlighted (by design, D53).
